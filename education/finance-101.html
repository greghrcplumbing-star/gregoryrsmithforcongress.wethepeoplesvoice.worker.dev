18 & Under · Family Money Education

Money & Life Skills

Young people deserve an early introduction to money, saving, budgeting, taxes, credit, investing awareness, and scam protection. These are basics too many students are expected to figure out only after mistakes become expensive.

Educational only: This page is not financial, tax, legal, or investment advice. Minors should learn with a parent, guardian, teacher, or qualified professional. Investing involves risk, and taxes should be verified with official sources or a tax professional.

What every young person should learn early

Budgeting Basics

Understand income, expenses, needs, wants, emergency savings, and why every dollar needs a job before it disappears.

Saving & Banking

Learn checking accounts, savings accounts, compound interest, debit cards, direct deposit, bank fees, and how to protect login information.

Taxes

Learn what taxes are, why paychecks have withholding, what W-2 and 1099 income mean, and why keeping records matters.

Credit

Learn how credit scores work, how debt grows, how minimum payments can trap people, and why payment history matters.

Investing Awareness

Learn the difference between saving, investing, speculation, risk, diversification, long-term thinking, and avoiding hype-driven decisions.

Scam Protection

Learn how fake giveaways, phishing links, pressure tactics, impersonators, and “guaranteed money” claims can steal from families.

Why this matters

Too many young people enter adulthood without knowing how a paycheck works, how taxes hit income, how credit can help or hurt them, how debt compounds, how scams target beginners, or how long-term investing differs from gambling. The goal is not to push kids into risky decisions. The goal is to give them vocabulary, confidence, caution, and practical awareness before the real world starts charging interest.

Age-appropriate learning path

Early introduction, not early pressure.

Ages 8–12

Needs vs. wants, saving goals, allowance tracking, simple budgeting, giving, chores, and basic bank safety.

Ages 13–15

First job basics, paychecks, taxes, debit cards, online safety, simple interest, compound interest, and avoiding scams.

Ages 16–18

Credit, car costs, job forms, filing basics, emergency funds, student loans, trade-school costs, and investing vocabulary.

Family discussion checklist

Before a First Job

  • How paychecks work
  • Why taxes are withheld
  • Where money should go first
  • How to keep personal records

Before a Bank Account

  • Debit card safety
  • Overdraft fees
  • Password protection
  • Trusted adults and account access

Before Investing

  • Risk comes first
  • No guaranteed returns
  • Diversification basics
  • Do not invest money needed for bills
